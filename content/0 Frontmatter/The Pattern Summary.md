# The Pattern Summary

> *All 123 patterns in one line each. Alexander put the equivalent list at the front of A Pattern Language, and it's the page readers actually use: it's how you find the pattern that names your problem without reading the book to get there.*

> [!note]- How to use this list
> Read it straight through once to see how the whole language fits together. After that, use it as an index: scan for the sentence that names the problem in front of you, open that pattern, then follow its **Larger** links upward to find the decision that caused your problem and its **Smaller** links downward to find the work.
>
> The line under each pattern is its tagline, the claim compressed to one sentence. It's a summary rather than the pattern itself; every one of them is an argument that needs its own essay, which is what the pattern is.
>
> **There is no confidence rating.** A pattern's standing here is whatever its Examples and cited sources show when you open it, so the index can't hand you that judgment; the page has to earn it. Alexander marked his patterns with asterisks, two down to none, to say how sure he was that each one named a true invariant. This book carries no equivalent mark.

## Part I · Market Structure

*36 patterns.*

### 01 Markets Are Made

**MS-001** &nbsp; [[MS-001 Designed Market, Not Found Market]]<br>A market is engineered by the position you take; segmentation only maps the ground someone else already owns.

**MS-002** &nbsp; [[MS-002 Market Type Sets the Playbook]]<br>A playbook borrowed from the wrong kind of market fails no matter how well you run it.

**MS-003** &nbsp; [[MS-003 Window of Inevitability]]<br>Every shift opens a window when a new approach is becoming inevitable but not yet obvious; too early you evangelize and starve, too late the door has closed.

**MS-004** &nbsp; [[MS-004 Narrative Precedes Demand]]<br>A market reads you before you speak, and correcting that read costs more than authoring it.

**MS-005** &nbsp; [[MS-005 Category Before Product]]<br>Build the shelf before you sell the book.

**MS-006** &nbsp; [[MS-006 Niche-to-Platform Gradient]]<br>Every product sits somewhere on the line from narrow wedge to broad platform and that position governs its pricing, its moat, and its path to expand.

**MS-007** &nbsp; [[MS-007 Maturity Kills Premium]]<br>A category pays its highest margins when it is young; as it matures, rivals converge and the premium bleeds toward cost. Act on the clock.

### 02 Who Buys

**MS-008** &nbsp; [[MS-008 Pre-Product Problem Interviews]]<br>Before you ship, the only instrument you have is a conversation, and most founders use it to pitch.

**MS-009** &nbsp; [[MS-009 Buyer Archetypes]]<br>The market keeps sending the same few kinds of people; learn them once and you'll recognize them everywhere.

**MS-010** &nbsp; [[MS-010 ICP Definition]]<br>A narrow, evidence-based portrait of the one customer everything else is built to serve.

**MS-011** &nbsp; [[MS-011 Negative ICP]]<br>Write down who you refuse to serve, or your funnel will decide for you.

**MS-012** &nbsp; [[MS-012 Buying Committee]]<br>Every seat at the table can say no, and each is afraid of something different.

**MS-013** &nbsp; [[MS-013 Champion, Not Contact]]<br>One insider who'll spend their own credibility for you beats a hundred friendly names in your CRM.

**MS-014** &nbsp; [[MS-014 Practitioner and Purchaser Are Different People]]<br>The person who loves your product and the person who pays for it are rarely the same, and they want opposite things.

**MS-015** &nbsp; [[MS-015 Buyers Come in Clusters]]<br>Buyers travel in tight groups that watch each other before any of them moves.

**MS-016** &nbsp; [[MS-016 Power Law Accounts]]<br>A few accounts decide whether you live; the rest is noise.

**MS-017** &nbsp; [[MS-017 Adoption Curve Ownership]]<br>Each segment of the adoption curve is a separate market, with its own buyer, its own message, and its own price.

### 03 Price Shapes the Market

**MS-018** &nbsp; [[MS-018 Price Creates the Buyer]]<br>Your messaging says "enterprise," but your price tag says "hobbyist," and the price tag always wins.

**MS-019** &nbsp; [[MS-019 Unit of Value]]<br>Find the one thing the buyer counts as value, and meter your price on that; everything else is a proxy that drifts.

**MS-020** &nbsp; [[MS-020 Profitability Envelope]]<br>Every market permits only a band of margin to survive; build a cost structure outside it and the math kills you, however good the product.

**MS-021** &nbsp; [[MS-021 Free Changes the Question]]<br>Free doesn't lower the price of the sale; it replaces the buying question with an adoption question, and that rewires everything.

### 04 Position and Territory

**MS-022** &nbsp; [[MS-022 Positioning as Contrast]]<br>A buyer learns what you are by understanding what you replace; choose that contrast, or the market will choose it for you.

**MS-023** &nbsp; [[MS-023 Category Creation]]<br>If no shelf fits you, build the shelf and teach the market its name.

**MS-024** &nbsp; [[MS-024 Category Entry Point]]<br>Nobody wakes up wanting your category; a situation pushes them into it, so own the situations.

**MS-025** &nbsp; [[MS-025 Competitive Geography]]<br>Your real competition is rarely the vendor you watch; it's the spreadsheet, the in-house build, and the decision to do nothing.

**MS-026** &nbsp; [[MS-026 Fast Follower Arrives]]<br>The moment your category works it becomes legible to everyone, and the education you paid for is sitting in the market for free.

**MS-027** &nbsp; [[MS-027 Economic Moat]]<br>Features win the first sale; a moat is why you still have the customer, and the margin, three years later.

**MS-028** &nbsp; [[MS-028 Data Gravity Lock-In]]<br>Become the place the data lives, and mass starts attracting mass: the more they store, the harder it is to leave.

**MS-029** &nbsp; [[MS-029 Trusted Neutral]]<br>Win by being the one party everyone can safely join; neutrality is a position a competitor cannot take without destroying it.

### 05 Sizing and Focus

**MS-030** &nbsp; [[MS-030 Sizing Is Storytelling]]<br>Build the market size as a story; the number is only as good as the argument behind it.

**MS-031** &nbsp; [[MS-031 Market Segmentation]]<br>Divide the market by how groups actually buy, then engage each on its own terms.

**MS-032** &nbsp; [[MS-032 Beachhead Market]]<br>To get big, think small: own one narrow segment completely before you reach for the next.

**MS-033** &nbsp; [[MS-033 Spear Fishing]]<br>When fifty accounts decide your year, stop trawling for leads and go spear the fifty by name.

**MS-034** &nbsp; [[MS-034 No Is the Most Profitable Word]]<br>The mismatched deal that pays this quarter costs you the focus that wins the market; the discipline is learning to refuse it.

### 06 Market and Money

**MS-035** &nbsp; [[MS-035 Go-To-Funding vs. Go-To-Market Balance]]<br>Make funding fuel a working engine; run your go-to-market to please investors instead of customers and you're burning cash you haven't earned.

**MS-036** &nbsp; [[MS-036 Unit Economics as Market Truth]]<br>Read CAC and LTV as the market telling you the truth about your structure; no campaign argues with the math.

## Part II · GTM Systems

*33 patterns.*

### 07 The Primary Motion

**GTM-001** &nbsp; [[GTM-001 Single Primary Motion]]<br>A company can run many supporting systems, but only one motion can lead; the rest fall in behind it or quietly fight it.

**GTM-002** &nbsp; [[GTM-002 PLG-SLG Hybrid]]<br>Let the product win the user and the salesperson win the company; the product lands the account and a human expands it when the usage says it's time.

**GTM-003** &nbsp; [[GTM-003 Founder-Led Sales]]<br>Before there's a playbook, the only person who can sell the product is the person who can speak like a co-founder, because that person is one.

**GTM-004** &nbsp; [[GTM-004 Repeatable Sales Motion]]<br>Don't build a sales team of artists. Turn your winning deals into a system a trained hire can run rather than one that needs a brilliant closer in the room.

### 08 First Contact

**GTM-005** &nbsp; [[GTM-005 Permissionless First Contact]]<br>Let a stranger reach value before anyone says yes.

**GTM-006** &nbsp; [[GTM-006 Generous Free Tier]]<br>Give away enough that a stranger gets real work done for free, and draw the paid line where their success starts to scale.

**GTM-007** &nbsp; [[GTM-007 Frictionless Onboarding]]<br>Every step between signup and first value is a place to lose the user. Cut the path to the fewest steps, time the trip, and carry the user past the first payoff into a habit.

### 09 The Demand Engine

**GTM-008** &nbsp; [[GTM-008 Demand Creation vs. Demand Capture]]<br>You can only harvest a field you planted; in a new market there's nothing yet to reap.

**GTM-009** &nbsp; [[GTM-009 Awareness Cascade]]<br>Don't buy attention with a megaphone. Earn it from a few trusted insiders and let it spread outward, one credible relationship at a time.

**GTM-010** &nbsp; [[GTM-010 Technical Content Marketing]]<br>A skeptical expert won't read your pitch; they'll read the thing that solves their problem, and that's who you get to be.

**GTM-011** &nbsp; [[GTM-011 Content as Lead Filter]]<br>A piece of content earns its keep by the people it turns away rather than the crowd it draws.

**GTM-012** &nbsp; [[GTM-012 Targeted Reputation Building]]<br>Don't try to be famous; be known and respected by the thousand people who can actually buy.

**GTM-013** &nbsp; [[GTM-013 Authority Amplification]]<br>A skeptical market won't trust your logo, but it will trust your people; make your experts visible and their credibility carries the company.

**GTM-014** &nbsp; [[GTM-014 Brand as Moat]]<br>A rival can copy your features by next quarter and outspend you on ads by Friday, but years of earned trust is the one advantage capital can't buy in a hurry.

**GTM-015** &nbsp; [[GTM-015 Dark Funnel Illumination]]<br>The buyer decided in a room you can't enter; instrument what leaks out of it and send in something worth carrying.

**GTM-016** &nbsp; [[GTM-016 Account-Based Everything]]<br>When a handful of accounts decide the year, aim the whole company at them; the marketing team can't carry it alone.

### 10 Pricing and Packaging Systems

**GTM-017** &nbsp; [[GTM-017 Usage-Based Pricing]]<br>Charge for what gets consumed, and a bill that starts at pennies grows only when the customer's success does.

**GTM-018** &nbsp; [[GTM-018 Good-Better-Best]]<br>Give the buyer three doors instead of a menu; each door names who it's for.

**GTM-019** &nbsp; [[GTM-019 Gating Line]]<br>Put the wall at the point where the user has felt the value and their own growth is pushing them past it; keep it clear of the user still trying to get hooked.

**GTM-020** &nbsp; [[GTM-020 Bundle Logic]]<br>Bundle a group the buyer already treats as one thing to win one budget; set the intense minority's capability off to the side as an add-on so it never taxes everyone else.

### 11 Channels and Ecosystem

**GTM-021** &nbsp; [[GTM-021 Practitioner-First GTM]]<br>In a technical market the person who adopts your product shows up years before the person who signs for it, so point the whole motion at them.

**GTM-022** &nbsp; [[GTM-022 Developer Advocacy]]<br>A skeptical practitioner audience trusts one of their own; hire one and let them speak.

**GTM-023** &nbsp; [[GTM-023 Public API Flywheel]]<br>Every developer who builds on your API becomes a distribution channel your sales team never had to hire.

**GTM-024** &nbsp; [[GTM-024 Partner Force Multipliers]]<br>A partner lends you their trust and their access, but only after you've proven you can sell without them.

**GTM-025** &nbsp; [[GTM-025 Marketplace Presence]]<br>Don't expect the listing to win the deal; use it to clear the procurement friction that stalls a deal you've already won.

### 12 The Growth Loop

**GTM-026** &nbsp; [[GTM-026 Growth Loop]]<br>A funnel spends a customer once; a loop makes each customer produce the next one, so growth compounds instead of running out the bottom.

**GTM-027** &nbsp; [[GTM-027 Product-Qualified Lead]]<br>A form-fill says someone downloaded a PDF; a product-qualified lead says someone already got value and hit the wall. Chase the second one.

**GTM-028** &nbsp; [[GTM-028 Sales-Assisted Conversion]]<br>The sales-assist rep opens with "I see you're setting up SSO, I can help," because the product already told them what the buyer needs.

**GTM-029** &nbsp; [[GTM-029 Land and Expand]]<br>Get in through a side door for almost nothing, then build the account you couldn't have sold on day one.

**GTM-030** &nbsp; [[GTM-030 Freemium-to-Enterprise]]<br>The people who already use you for free are the pipeline; the enterprise sale is for everything the company needs that they never asked for.

**GTM-031** &nbsp; [[GTM-031 Support Is Sales]]<br>In a business that lives on renewals, every support ticket either protects the next renewal or quietly seeds the next churn.

**GTM-032** &nbsp; [[GTM-032 Customer Success as Growth Engine]]<br>In a subscription business the money you already won is the market you haven't sold yet; whoever owns the installed base owns most of next year's revenue.

**GTM-033** &nbsp; [[GTM-033 Expansion Before Acquisition]]<br>The cheapest next dollar is already inside a customer you have; grow the base before you go buy a new one.

## Part III · Operations

*24 patterns.*

### 13 Sensemaking

**OPS-001** &nbsp; [[OPS-001 Marketing as Sensemaking]]<br>Make the company understand what the market is telling it before you make it louder.

**OPS-002** &nbsp; [[OPS-002 Win-Loss Discipline]]<br>Every deal already ran the experiment; refuse to throw the result away.

**OPS-003** &nbsp; [[OPS-003 Feedback Before Scale]]<br>Scale multiplies whatever you've understood, so close the loop before you pour.

**OPS-004** &nbsp; [[OPS-004 Closing the Loop]]<br>A loop is only closed when an insight changes a decision and the person who gave you the insight hears back.

### 14 The Engine Room

**OPS-005** &nbsp; [[OPS-005 Funnel Stages, Yours]]<br>A funnel stage is a promise about the buyer's reality; borrow someone else's and you forecast a journey your buyer never takes.

**OPS-006** &nbsp; [[OPS-006 CRM as Shared Memory]]<br>What one person knows about an account leaves with that person; what the system knows survives them.

**OPS-007** &nbsp; [[OPS-007 Lead Scoring and Routing]]<br>A ranked list works itself; an unranked one works whoever shouts loudest. Score the leads by who's ready, then hand each one to the right person before the readiness fades.

**OPS-008** &nbsp; [[OPS-008 Nurture Architecture]]<br>Most of the demand you create isn't ready yet; nurture is the patient system that keeps it warm until it is.

**OPS-009** &nbsp; [[OPS-009 Messaging Cadence]]<br>Say one thing, and say it on rhythm: a message the buyer sees once is a message the buyer never saw.

**OPS-010** &nbsp; [[OPS-010 Minimum Viable Martech Stack]]<br>Four jobs need covering; every tool beyond the fourth has to prove a job nobody else is doing.

**OPS-011** &nbsp; [[OPS-011 Data Warehouse Decisions]]<br>The grain you store today is the finest question you'll be able to ask next year.

### 15 Content Operations

**OPS-012** &nbsp; [[OPS-012 Content as Capital Asset]]<br>A campaign is spent the day it runs; an asset keeps paying you back for years.

**OPS-013** &nbsp; [[OPS-013 Repurposing Pipeline]]<br>Put the work into the anchor once, then make it pay back ten times.

**OPS-014** &nbsp; [[OPS-014 Editorial Cadence]]<br>An audience trusts the schedule before it trusts the writer; publish on a rhythm you can hold and the trust compounds, skip the rhythm and it drains.

**OPS-015** &nbsp; [[OPS-015 Documentation First]]<br>For a technical buyer, the docs are where they meet the product, try it, and decide; build them that way.

### 16 Team and Rhythm

**OPS-016** &nbsp; [[OPS-016 Generalists Then Specialists]]<br>Hire for range first; earn the right to hire for depth only once the motion is worth dividing.

**OPS-017** &nbsp; [[OPS-017 Sales as Narrative Reinforcement]]<br>Every sales call retells your company's story one more time; make sure every rep tells it well.

**OPS-018** &nbsp; [[OPS-018 Sales Enablement Architecture]]<br>A rep performs to the quality of the system that arms them. Give one rep a system and ten reps a shrug, and you'll get one good quarter and nine excuses.

**OPS-019** &nbsp; [[OPS-019 Quarterly Recalibration]]<br>The assumptions that were right on day one go stale in silence; put them on the calendar so the company changes course on purpose rather than by accident.

### 17 Measurement

**OPS-020** &nbsp; [[OPS-020 Metrics That Change Behavior]]<br>Put a number on the dashboard only when some value of it would make you do something different.

**OPS-021** &nbsp; [[OPS-021 Leading and Lagging Indicators]]<br>A lagging metric tells you the score after the game; pair it with a signal that moves while you can still change the outcome.

**OPS-022** &nbsp; [[OPS-022 Cohort Analysis as Decision-Making]]<br>A blended average is the sound of two trends canceling out; a cohort is either one, heard on its own.

**OPS-023** &nbsp; [[OPS-023 Health Scores]]<br>A composite score is a prediction; if nobody checks it against what happened, it's a mood with a number attached.

**OPS-024** &nbsp; [[OPS-024 Friction Audit]]<br>Analytics tell you where they left; only walking it yourself tells you why.

## Part IV · Craft

*30 patterns.*

### 18 Message and Word

**CR-001** &nbsp; [[CR-001 Atomic Value Prop]]<br>If you can't say what you do in one sentence a stranger repeats correctly, you don't have a value prop; you have a pile of features waiting for a sentence.

**CR-002** &nbsp; [[CR-002 Positioning Statement]]<br>A form with seven blanks and no room to hedge: fill it honestly and you'll discover which decisions your company hasn't made.

**CR-003** &nbsp; [[CR-003 Naming Things]]<br>The name is the piece of positioning you'll repeat a million times, so it had better argue your case while you sleep.

**CR-004** &nbsp; [[CR-004 Message Pillars and Proof Points]]<br>Three claims, each nailed to something a stranger could check; a pillar with nothing under it is an opinion in a suit.

**CR-005** &nbsp; [[CR-005 Persona Messaging]]<br>Same claim, different first sentence: change what you lead with per buyer, and change nothing about what's true.

**CR-006** &nbsp; [[CR-006 Objection-Handling Narratives]]<br>Say the objection out loud before the buyer does, and you get to be the one who frames it.

**CR-007** &nbsp; [[CR-007 One Message Per Asset]]<br>A surface that argues three things is remembered for none of them.

**CR-008** &nbsp; [[CR-008 Specificity Signals Truth]]<br>A number a buyer can check reads as honest; a superlative nobody can check reads as noise.

**CR-009** &nbsp; [[CR-009 Jargon Budget]]<br>Every specialist word is a withdrawal from an account the reader can overdraw; spend it where it buys precision or belonging, and nowhere else.

**CR-010** &nbsp; [[CR-010 Message Testing]]<br>The message is a hypothesis until a stranger you've never met decides to click.

### 19 Story and Proof

**CR-011** &nbsp; [[CR-011 Buyer's Journey Narrative]]<br>Nobody wakes up wanting your product; they wake up with a problem they can't yet name, and your job is to tell them the rest of the story in order.

**CR-012** &nbsp; [[CR-012 Teach the Buyer to Buy]]<br>A new-category buyer must assemble a purchase no one has shown them how to make.

**CR-013** &nbsp; [[CR-013 Data-Driven Storytelling]]<br>A number you gathered yourself is a story no competitor can tell.

**CR-014** &nbsp; [[CR-014 Social Proof Architecture]]<br>A logo wall at the bottom of the page reassures nobody; a single customer quote beside the exact sentence a buyer doubts changes the sale.

**CR-015** &nbsp; [[CR-015 Demo Script]]<br>The buyer remembers the story your demo told and forgets every feature it listed.

**CR-016** &nbsp; [[CR-016 ROI Narrative]]<br>A buyer trusts a number they helped compute and discounts one you brought on a slide.

### 20 Assets and Surfaces

**CR-017** &nbsp; [[CR-017 Visual Hierarchy]]<br>The eye reads in an order you either chose or surrendered; visual hierarchy is choosing it.

**CR-018** &nbsp; [[CR-018 Homepage Before-After-Why]]<br>A visitor doesn't want a tour of your product; they want to see a better version of their own life and one reason to believe it.

**CR-019** &nbsp; [[CR-019 One-Pager]]<br>If it doesn't fit on one page, you haven't decided what you're arguing yet.

**CR-020** &nbsp; [[CR-020 Social Media Playbook]]<br>Every platform has a house style; pay it the courtesy of its native form while you keep telling the one story.

**CR-021** &nbsp; [[CR-021 Video and Podcast Craft]]<br>An hour of your voice, given willingly, is a claim on the buyer's trust that no thirty-second ad can make.

### 21 Conversion Craft

**CR-022** &nbsp; [[CR-022 Friction as Qualification]]<br>The step you make people climb is the question you're asking them, and only the ones who answer are worth talking to.

**CR-023** &nbsp; [[CR-023 Call to Conversation, Not Conversion]]<br>A form asks the buyer to commit before they've decided; a question lets them decide by talking to you.

**CR-024** &nbsp; [[CR-024 Form as Filter]]<br>Every field is a toll booth, and some of them charge more than the lead is worth.

**CR-025** &nbsp; [[CR-025 Experiment Design]]<br>A test that can't change what you ship is a survey with better production values.

**CR-026** &nbsp; [[CR-026 A-B Testing Ethics]]<br>A test can win the metric and lose the customer; the buyer you tricked into clicking is the buyer who leaves.

### 22 Competitive Craft

**CR-027** &nbsp; [[CR-027 Differentiation Axis]]<br>Pick the ruler the market measures you with, or the incumbent will hand you theirs and you'll lose on it.

**CR-028** &nbsp; [[CR-028 Comparison Page]]<br>The buyer is already comparing you; the only question is whether they do it on your page or on a page you don't control.

**CR-029** &nbsp; [[CR-029 Battle Card]]<br>A battle card that can't be read in the ten seconds before a rep answers the question has already lost the deal.

**CR-030** &nbsp; [[CR-030 Pricing Page Transparency]]<br>The price you refuse to show is still the loudest line on the page.
