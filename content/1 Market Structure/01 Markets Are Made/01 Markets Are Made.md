# Markets Are Made

Building a technical or enterprise market begins with a choice about the field itself. You can enter a settled market whose buyers already know the category and its alternatives, or define a new frame around a change buyers haven't organized yet. [[MS-001 Designed Market, Not Found Market]] owns that choice. The six patterns around it identify the market type and enabling shift. They then address the buyer's interpretation and category before testing breadth against the maturity clock.

This chapter settles whether the company will accept an existing buying frame or author one. Market design remains bounded by evidence. A real shift has to support the claim, and buyers have to recognize the problem. The economics must also survive as competitors arrive.

## From Market Choice to Maturity

| Pattern | Use in the sequence |
|---|---|
| [[MS-001 Designed Market, Not Found Market]] | Decide whether you're entering a defined market or designing the frame in which buyers will judge you. |
| [[MS-002 Market Type Sets the Playbook]] | Classify the market type and set the first-cycle objective that follows from it. |
| [[MS-003 Window of Inevitability]] | Test whether an enabling shift has made the new approach credible and whether the opening remains available. |
| [[MS-004 Narrative Precedes Demand]] | Give buyers an interpretation of the shift before scattered facts harden into a cheaper reading. |
| [[MS-005 Category Before Product]] | Name the mental shelf that determines the comparison set and buying path. |
| [[MS-006 Niche-to-Platform Gradient]] | Locate the offer between a narrow wedge and a broad platform, then identify the adjacent move it can support. |
| [[MS-007 Maturity Kills Premium]] | Read how convergence changes the available premium and the urgency of building durable holds. |

The sequence is iterative. A weak timing test can force a narrower category. Buyer response can change the market-type reading. Maturity can move a platform toward a more focused position. The patterns give each revision a named decision.

## Boundaries Inside Market Design

[[MS-001 Designed Market, Not Found Market]] owns the founding choice. [[MS-023 Category Creation]] later owns the sustained work of installing a category in institutions and buyer language. [[MS-005 Category Before Product]] covers the earlier filing decision a buyer makes when the product first appears.

[[MS-002 Market Type Sets the Playbook]] determines which market type you're in and what your first planning cycle must accomplish. [[MS-003 Window of Inevitability]] tests readiness and timing. [[GTM-008 Demand Creation vs. Demand Capture]] later determines the order of spending once that type is known.

Narrative and category also do separate work. [[MS-004 Narrative Precedes Demand]] explains what changed and why the buyer should care. [[MS-005 Category Before Product]] determines where the buyer files the offer, which changes the alternatives they consider.

## Handoff to Buyer Structure

[[02 Who Buys]] inherits a market claim placed in a category, along with its timing assumption. It tests whose problem is acute and who can approve a purchase. It also identifies the groups that can carry adoption. A designed market becomes operational only after that buyer structure is specific enough to guide price and motion.
