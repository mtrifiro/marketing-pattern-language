# Who Buys

A market claim becomes useful when it names the people and organizations whose behavior can confirm it. This chapter replaces a broad audience with an evidence-backed buyer system. It settles whom you're building for and who enters first. It then identifies who can approve the purchase and where attention should concentrate.

Technical and enterprise purchases often split use from authority. A practitioner can create adoption while a purchaser controls budget, and several additional seats can stop the deal. The patterns below keep committee roles separate from peer groups and market segments.

## From Buyer Evidence to Market Weight

| Pattern | Use in the sequence |
|---|---|
| [[MS-008 Pre-Product Problem Interviews]] | Gather buyer language and decision evidence before product behavior exists. |
| [[MS-009 Buyer Archetypes]] | Name the recurring motivation-and-constraint types that appear across accounts. |
| [[MS-010 ICP Definition]] | Select the company and person the whole go-to-market will be built to serve. |
| [[MS-011 Negative ICP]] | Write the disqualifying conditions that keep poor-fit demand from redirecting the company. |
| [[MS-012 Buying Committee]] | Map every seat in a consequential purchase and the power it holds. |
| [[MS-013 Champion, Not Contact]] | Identify the insider who will spend credibility and carry the case into rooms you can't enter. |
| [[MS-014 Practitioner and Purchaser Are Different People]] | Decide which of the two enters first and build the bridge to the other before the sale depends on it. |
| [[MS-015 Buyers Come in Clusters]] | Concentrate adoption inside peer groups whose members trust one another's choices. |
| [[MS-016 Power Law Accounts]] | Weight accounts by the revenue and reference value they can actually contribute. |
| [[MS-017 Adoption Curve Ownership]] | Treat each readiness segment as a distinct market and rebuild the offer at each crossing. |

Pre-product interviews are the entry point when usage data and completed deals don't exist. Once the product is live, the same model should absorb observed adoption and completed-deal evidence. The source changes; the need for a precise buyer model remains.

## Keep the Buyer Models Separate

An archetype describes a recurring person through motivation and constraint. An ICP selects the company-person combination that deserves the company's focus. [[MS-031 Market Segmentation]] groups buyers whose behavior requires a different treatment. Each model answers a different planning question.

A champion is identified by what they do inside a purchase. Practitioner and purchaser are roles defined by use and authority. The champion may be a practitioner; advocacy requires evidence beyond friendly product use.

[[MS-015 Buyers Come in Clusters]] tracks peer trust. [[MS-016 Power Law Accounts]] tracks unequal account value. [[MS-017 Adoption Curve Ownership]] tracks readiness as a market develops.

## Handoff to Price

[[03 Price Shapes the Market]] inherits a chosen buyer and a mapped purchase. Price can then call the intended procurement path into existence. A price that changes the buyer or procurement group exposes an unresolved choice in this chapter.
