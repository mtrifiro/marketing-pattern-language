# Price Shapes the Market

Price determines who can buy and how approval happens. It also limits the cost structure the business can carry. This chapter treats price as market structure before the number reaches a buyer-facing surface. The four patterns settle the price band and value unit, then test the permitted margin and the choice to make the first offer free.

These decisions constrain one another. A high price calls a larger buying group into the deal. A weak value unit makes expansion feel arbitrary. A cost structure outside the available margin turns each new customer into a larger loss. Free changes who enters first and moves the hard work from acquisition to paid conversion.

## Keep the Commercial Decisions Separate

[[MS-018 Price Creates the Buyer]] owns the amount and the buyer it calls. [[MS-019 Unit of Value]] owns the dimension along which value is counted. [[GTM-017 Usage-Based Pricing]] owns one billing model that charges as consumption of that unit rises. A company can choose the right value unit and still choose a billing model that collects it poorly.

[[MS-020 Profitability Envelope]] describes the structural band the market allows before the business commits to a cost base. [[MS-036 Unit Economics as Market Truth]] later reads the actual results and traces a broken number to the upstream decision that produced it.

[[MS-021 Free Changes the Question]] owns the market-level decision to let adoption come first. [[GTM-006 Generous Free Tier]] defines a free offer that delivers enough value to support adoption. [[GTM-019 Gating Line]] determines where success creates a reason to pay.

## Handoff to Position

[[04 Position and Territory]] inherits a priced buyer and a defined value unit. Those choices narrow the alternatives against which the offer will be judged and the kinds of defense the economics can fund. Position can raise the structural ceiling only by changing the comparison or making the next-best option materially worse.
