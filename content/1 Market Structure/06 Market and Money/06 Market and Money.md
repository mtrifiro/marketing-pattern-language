# Market and Money

Part I ends by testing its market choices against money. A company can raise capital because investors believe the opportunity is large, while its customer economics still fail. It can also protect short-term efficiency so tightly that it never funds the work required to build a new category. This chapter gives those two systems separate readouts and asks the operating plan to reconcile them.

The decision is how much company behavior will be set by the next financing event and how much will be set by evidence from customers. Capital can fund the route; unit economics show whether the route is producing a business that can support itself.

## Two Financial Readouts

| Pattern | Decision it owns |
|---|---|
| [[MS-035 Go-To-Funding vs. Go-To-Market Balance]] | Separate the plan built to raise capital from the plan built to acquire and retain customers, then state where they depend on each other. |
| [[MS-036 Unit Economics as Market Truth]] | Read the five unit-economic measures together as evidence about the market structure chosen upstream. |

These patterns work as a pair. The first identifies which audience a plan is written to satisfy and which clock it follows. The second checks what the operating system produces once customers move through it. A funding narrative can buy time for category education; it can't make a structurally poor customer profitable.

## Keep the Diagnosis Upstream

Financing and revenue are legitimate systems with different audiences and clocks. [[MS-035 Go-To-Funding vs. Go-To-Market Balance]] prevents an investor objective from passing silently into the customer plan. Every plan assumption should say which system requires it and what evidence will settle the bet.

[[MS-036 Unit Economics as Market Truth]] is broader than a campaign scorecard. A high acquisition cost can trace to the priced buyer or primary motion. Weak retention can trace to customer fit or an absent hold. The combined readout directs the inquiry; the upstream patterns identify the decision that needs revision.
