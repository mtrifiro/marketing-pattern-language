# First Contact

The first encounter has one job: let the intended entrant reach enough value to continue. It may happen in a product or another surface a buyer can inspect before a seller joins. This chapter settles who can enter and what they can experience for free. It then limits the setup that precedes value.

The three patterns form one sequence from accessible evaluation through a useful free experience to a specific value event. Each step should be designed for the primary motion chosen in [[07 The Primary Motion]].

## From Access to Repeated Use

| Pattern | Use in the sequence |
|---|---|
| [[GTM-005 Permissionless First Contact]] | Give the intended entrant a useful way to evaluate value without waiting for vendor approval. |
| [[GTM-006 Generous Free Tier]] | Make the free experience complete enough for the target user to succeed before the paid boundary appears. |
| [[GTM-007 Frictionless Onboarding]] | Remove every step that doesn't contribute to one named value event, then measure the time required to reach it. |

The chapter applies most directly when a practitioner can enter before a purchaser. In a high-touch sale, permissionless first contact may live in the technical proof available before the meeting. The primary motion still decides how much of the evaluation the product must carry alone.

## Where Entry Decisions Split

Permissionless access and a free price are separate decisions. [[GTM-005 Permissionless First Contact]] owns the ability to begin a meaningful evaluation. [[MS-021 Free Changes the Question]] owns the market-level choice to let adoption precede purchase. [[GTM-006 Generous Free Tier]] defines the useful offer that implements that choice.

[[GTM-019 Gating Line]] later decides which limit creates a reason to pay after the user has experienced value. The free tier establishes the experience; the gate establishes the upgrade boundary.

[[GTM-007 Frictionless Onboarding]] designs the shortest path to first value and continued use. [[OPS-024 Friction Audit]] later walks that path as a buyer and records what the behavioral data can't explain.

## Handoff to Demand

[[09 The Demand Engine]] inherits a first encounter that can keep its promise. It can then decide how trusted attention reaches the accounts that fit. Sending more people toward an unresolved first-value path would amplify the wrong problem.
