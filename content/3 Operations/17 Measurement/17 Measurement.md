# Measurement

A measurement system earns its cost when a number can change a decision. This chapter settles which numbers belong in view and how early signals connect to later outcomes. It also identifies when a team has to inspect the buyer's path directly. The aim is a small decision system whose measures have owners and prescribed responses.

The sequence moves from admission rule to diagnosis. The first pattern qualifies every metric. An early signal is then paired with an outcome, while cohort analysis follows comparable groups through time. A calibrated account score brings the view down to one customer. The final pattern walks the experience that those measures can locate but can't explain.

## From Decision Rule to Direct Inspection

| Pattern | Use in the sequence |
|---|---|
| [[OPS-020 Metrics That Change Behavior]] | Keep a metric only when a plausible value would change a named decision. |
| [[OPS-021 Leading and Lagging Indicators]] | Pair an outcome with an earlier signal and verify that the relationship holds in historical data. |
| [[OPS-022 Cohort Analysis as Decision-Making]] | Follow comparable groups through time when a blended average would hide a change that matters. |
| [[OPS-023 Health Scores]] | Create a composite account score calibrated against realized outcomes, with each band wired to an action. |
| [[OPS-024 Friction Audit]] | Walk the buyer's path on a schedule and record the specific obstruction behind a measured drop. |

Each view answers a different question. Adding more views without a decision attached recreates the dashboard clutter the first pattern removes.

## What Each Measure Can Answer

[[OPS-021 Leading and Lagging Indicators]] owns the verified relationship between an earlier signal and a later outcome. [[OPS-023 Health Scores]] owns a composite account score, its calibration against realized renewal or expansion, and the action assigned to each band.

[[OPS-022 Cohort Analysis as Decision-Making]] compares groups through the same stage of their life. A health score estimates the condition of one account at a given time. Cohort analysis can test whether a price or channel change improved a population; the score can trigger work on a specific customer.

[[OPS-024 Friction Audit]] supplies direct observation. A measured drop identifies where to look. Walking the path can reveal the confusing screen or broken handoff behind it.

[[MS-036 Unit Economics as Market Truth]] owns the joint reading of its five unit-economic measures. This chapter governs how the operation keeps decision-grade measures current and interpretable.

## Handoff to Craft

[[18 Message and Word]] inherits evidence about buyer hesitation and departure. Part IV turns that evidence into a revised claim and carries the claim onto buyer-facing surfaces. Measurement locates the point that deserves work; craft changes what the buyer meets there.
