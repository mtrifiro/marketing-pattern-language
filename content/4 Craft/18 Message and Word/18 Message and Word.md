# Message and Word

Part IV begins where the market strategy has to survive compression into language a buyer can understand and repeat. This chapter settles the stable claim and the internal statement that supports it. The remaining disciplines carry that language across audiences and assets. Writing also tests the upstream strategy: an unresolved buyer or comparison becomes visible when the team can't complete one clear sentence.

The ten patterns work as a language system. [[CR-001 Atomic Value Prop]] is the root. Some patterns expand that sentence into support or buyer-specific versions. Others constrain what a single surface may say and how much specialist vocabulary it can spend. Naming compresses the position into the word the market repeats most. Testing lets buyer behavior decide which language survives.

## From Stable Claim to Market Test

| Pattern | Use in the sequence |
|---|---|
| [[CR-001 Atomic Value Prop]] | Commit to one stable sentence that names the buyer and the change while giving a reason to choose the offer. |
| [[CR-002 Positioning Statement]] | Fill the internal strategic blanks until the target and comparison are explicit enough to support public language. |
| [[CR-003 Naming Things]] | Choose a name that reinforces the intended category and comparison every time someone repeats it. |
| [[CR-004 Message Pillars and Proof Points]] | Expand the central claim into a small set of supporting claims, each tied to checkable evidence. |
| [[CR-005 Persona Messaging]] | Recast the consequence and proof for a buyer archetype while keeping the product claim fixed. |
| [[CR-006 Objection-Handling Narratives]] | Prepare a short answer for each doubt that repeatedly decides a deal. |
| [[CR-007 One Message Per Asset]] | Give each asset one claim and one action a cold reader can repeat. |
| [[CR-008 Specificity Signals Truth]] | Replace vague assurance with a detail the reader can verify independently. |
| [[CR-009 Jargon Budget]] | Limit specialist terms according to the reader's fluency and the asset's depth. |
| [[CR-010 Message Testing]] | Put proposed language in front of real buyers and retain it only when their behavior supports it. |

The table is a working sequence, though testing begins before the system is complete. A positioning statement or name that fails with buyers should reopen the decision that produced it. Stability means holding a supported claim long enough to compound; it doesn't protect a claim that evidence has rejected.

## Keep the Language Layers Separate

[[MS-022 Positioning as Contrast]] chooses the alternative against which the offer should be understood. [[CR-002 Positioning Statement]] records the internal strategic answer. [[CR-001 Atomic Value Prop]] compresses that answer into the stable claim the company carries publicly.

The atomic value proposition is the central sentence. [[CR-004 Message Pillars and Proof Points]] gives that sentence a supporting structure. [[CR-007 One Message Per Asset]] limits each surface to the one part it can carry well.

[[MS-009 Buyer Archetypes]] supplies durable buyer types. [[CR-005 Persona Messaging]] adapts the consequence and evidence for one of those types. It preserves the underlying claim so the company doesn't become a different product for every audience.

Specificity and jargon solve different problems. [[CR-008 Specificity Signals Truth]] makes a claim checkable. [[CR-009 Jargon Budget]] controls the cost of specialist language. A precise number can be plain; a technical term can be exact and still exclude the intended reader.

[[CR-010 Message Testing]] owns the question of whether the words work with a buyer. [[CR-025 Experiment Design]] later owns the rigor of a test built to settle a decision.

## Handoff to Story and Proof

[[19 Story and Proof]] inherits a stable claim and its evidence. It arranges them across the buyer's journey and teaches an unfamiliar purchase. Each point of doubt receives the proof it requires. A sentence becomes persuasive over time only when the next piece answers the next question the buyer is ready to ask.
