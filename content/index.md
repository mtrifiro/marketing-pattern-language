Every great market was built by someone. The categories that now feel like geography — CRM, cloud computing, ride-sharing, plant-based meat — each began as one company's claim about what had changed in the world, repeated until buyers, analysts, and competitors treated the claim as terrain.

This is a language for making that happen deliberately: **123 patterns** across four parts, each naming a situation you will recognize, the pressures that keep the obvious response from working, and a recommendation that holds.

## Start here

- **[[Introduction]]** — what this is, who it's for, and how to read it
- **[[The Pattern Summary]]** — all 123 patterns compressed to one line each, in order. The fastest way to find the pattern that names the problem in front of you.

Dependency flows downhill through the first three parts. At the craft level it runs both directions, because a value proposition that will not compress into one sentence is usually a positioning problem surfacing at the keyboard.

## Three ways in

**Front to back.** The language unfolds in the order a market gets built: structure first, craft last.

**From a Monday problem.** A homepage that will not convert, a demo that wanders — find its pattern in Part IV, then climb the **Larger** links until you reach the structural decision your craft problem is expressing.

**By role.** A founder begins in Part I, an operations lead in Part III, a writer in Part IV. Every path crosses the others, because the network connects them.

Each pattern is written to be opened cold; five minutes gives you the situation, the recommendation, and the cases behind it. Use the graph view to wander the network directly.
